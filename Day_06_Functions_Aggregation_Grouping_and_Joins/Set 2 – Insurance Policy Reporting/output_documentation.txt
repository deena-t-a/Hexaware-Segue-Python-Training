WHERE vs HAVING (explained with Query 3)
----------------------------------------
WHERE filters rows before they are grouped. HAVING filters the groups after
GROUP BY and COUNT have been calculated.

In Query 3:
  - WHERE p.status = 'Active' removes the Expired policy rows first.
    This leaves 11 of the 12 policies.
  - GROUP BY then counts the remaining policies per customer.
  - HAVING COUNT(*) > 1 keeps only customers whose count is above 1.

HAVING cannot be replaced by WHERE here because the count does not exist
until the rows are grouped. WHERE cannot be replaced by HAVING either, without
changing the answer: Elango M would be counted with 2 policies and wrongly listed.
